# Multi-Factor Authentication

## 1. Scope

Multi-factor authentication (MFA) is mandatory for corporate e-mail, remote
access, the finance applications, and any administrative account. It is
optional, and recommended, for everything else.

## 2. Accepted factors

Three factors are accepted, in this order of preference:

1. the authenticator application on a corporate or personal mobile device,
2. a hardware security key, issued on request to staff who cannot install the
   application,
3. a one-time code sent by SMS to the number registered in the directory.

SMS codes are being retired: they are refused for administrative accounts and
for the finance applications from 1 September 2026.

## 3. Enrolment

New joiners enrol during their first sign-in, guided by the on-screen prompt.
Enrolment must be completed within 5 working days of the account being created;
after that the account is restricted to the internal network until MFA is
enrolled.

Enrolment requires no service desk involvement. If the on-screen prompt does
not appear, the account is missing its MFA licence assignment, which the
service desk fixes.

## 4. Lost, replaced or wiped device

An MFA factor is re-enrolled, never transferred. When a device is lost,
replaced or wiped, the existing factor is revoked and the user enrols again.

Re-enrolment is a privileged action and follows the same two-proof identity rule
as a password reset (see the Password and Account Policy). A line-manager
confirmation is required when the request arrives from outside the corporate
network.

A temporary bypass code may be issued for a maximum of 8 hours when the user is
travelling and cannot enrol immediately. Bypass codes are issued by a service
desk supervisor, are single-use, and are recorded with the justification.

## 5. Frequency of prompts

On a compliant corporate device on the corporate network, a factor is requested
once every 7 days. On an unmanaged device, or from outside the corporate
network, a factor is requested at every sign-in.

Users who are prompted at every sign-in on a corporate device are checked for a
device that has fallen out of compliance, which is the usual cause.

## 6. What to do about unexpected prompts

An MFA prompt the user did not trigger means someone else holds their password.
The user must decline the prompt, change their password immediately, and report
it to security@nawa.qa. The service desk treats these reports as priority 2 and
never as a routine password reset.

## 7. Exemptions

Permanent exemptions are not granted. Where a role genuinely cannot use any
accepted factor, the IT Security Office issues a time-limited exception of at
most 90 days, reviewed by the account's line manager before any renewal.
